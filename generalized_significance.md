# 1.5 Significance of the Project

The proposed system is significant as an information-management intervention for local disaster-reporting chains and as a reusable digital service for participating disaster risk reduction and management organizations. Its purpose is not to automate official judgment or guarantee faster emergency response. It provides authorized field reporters, barangay personnel, organization administrators, verifiers, approvers, and viewers with a responsive web workflow for capturing, reviewing, correcting, consolidating, generating, and retrieving disaster records. The startup operates the technical platform, but each organization retains authority over its jurisdiction, users, validation workflow, approved reports, disclosure decisions, and official submissions. The system's value therefore depends on adoption, connectivity, data quality, training, institutional capacity, service governance, and successful evaluation in each participating setting.

## 1.5.1 Significance to Stakeholders

### Purok Personnel

Purok personnel are closest to localized incidents but may report through messages whose structure varies by sender. A mobile-browser-compatible form can guide them to provide the same essential categories for every incident, including location, time, observed hazard, affected people, damage, immediate needs, and actions already taken. Required fields and visible submission status may reduce omitted details and uncertainty about whether a report reached the barangay. The benefit should be evaluated through first-pass acceptance, task-success rate, time to submit, correction requests, and user-reported difficulty. The system remains connectivity-dependent and does not replace calls, radio, or other emergency channels when the web service is unavailable.

Emergency information systems operate within established responsibilities, communication practices, and trust relationships. Representative Purok and barangay personnel should therefore participate in requirements validation, scenario testing, training, and post-pilot review. Continued use cannot be inferred from technical availability alone. Research on inter-agency emergency systems has shown that information-sharing and coordination problems are organizational as well as technical, which supports evaluating workflow fit and user participation rather than assuming that a standardized form will automatically be adopted (Aedo et al., 2010).

### Barangay Personnel and the BDRRMC

Barangay personnel can receive Purok submissions in one jurisdiction-specific queue, review their provenance and status, request corrections, add barangay-level assessment data, and forward a structured record through the participating organization's configured workflow. This may reduce repeated transcription and make preliminary, returned, rejected, and verified reports easier to distinguish. Historical records may also support later review of affected households and actions taken. The expected benefit arises from shared fields, validation, status tracking, and version history, not merely from converting a paper form into a screen. It should be assessed through verification turnaround, duplicate and omission rates, correction effort, backlog, and successful retrieval of prior records.

The significance of structured barangay records extends beyond faster encoding. Consistent geographic units, hazard categories, timestamps, report states, impact fields, and source metadata can make later aggregation and comparison more defensible, provided local definitions are mapped to the applicable reporting standard and authorized personnel validate the source information. UNDRR identifies accurate, consistent, comparable, and disaggregated loss data as a basis for monitoring and risk-informed planning. The platform can support these qualities through structure and provenance, but it cannot establish the truth of an observation without appropriate local verification (UNDRR, n.d.).

### Participating DRRM Organizations and Local Decision-Makers

Authorized personnel in a participating provincial, city, municipal, barangay, or other authorized DRRM organization can review submissions with recorded sources and timestamps, document validation decisions, consolidate only eligible records, and trace generated SitRep values back to contributing local data. A searchable catalog and organization-scoped dashboard can support monitoring of report volume, status, affected-population totals, and processing activity. Controlled configuration allows each organization to assign its own reporters, reviewers, approvers, jurisdictions, terminology, and report routes without receiving a separate application codebase.

This may improve the consistency and auditability of information presented to local decision-makers, but the system does not determine incident legitimacy, allocate rescue resources, declare a state of calamity, or independently establish compliance. Those remain institutional decisions governed by law and official procedure. ZCDRRMO may serve as an original study setting or pilot organization, but it is not the platform's only possible administrative organization or authority (Republic Act No. 10121, 2010; NDRRMC, 2024).

### Startup and Platform Personnel

For the startup, the project provides a reusable service model rather than a custom application for one office. A common codebase can distribute improvements in hosting, security, accessibility, reporting, and maintenance across participating organizations while controlled configuration preserves local workflows. Platform administrators can onboard organizations, maintain service configuration, monitor availability, deploy updates, and provide technical support. They should not validate incidents, approve local reports, or exercise the statutory functions of customer DRRM personnel.

The startup's significance also depends on responsibilities that do not appear in a single-client prototype. These include service monitoring, security updates, support capacity, backup restoration, incident communication, authorized subprocessor management, portability, and orderly offboarding. A technically reusable platform is not automatically a viable public-sector product. Adoption remains subject to procurement, appropriations, contractual allocation of duties, customer oversight, provider performance, and value-for-money assessment under applicable government rules (Republic Act No. 12009, 2024).

The reusable model can reduce duplicated maintenance only if the provider remains capable of serving multiple organizations without weakening isolation, responsiveness, or local control. Conversely, a provider outage, compromise, insolvency, staffing failure, or abrupt termination can create correlated risk across customers. Its public value therefore includes tested restoration, integrity-checked customer exports, documented formats and data dictionaries, transition assistance, access revocation, subprocessor controls, and records-law-compatible treatment of retained copies. These controls make dependence visible and reducible; they do not eliminate it.

### Affected Communities

Communities may benefit indirectly when local needs are represented through more complete, consistently categorized, and traceable records. Better-organized information can support authorized officials' situational awareness and prioritization, but the project should not claim that the application by itself accelerates rescue, releases funds, prevents casualties, or guarantees equitable relief. Those outcomes depend on verification, organizational capacity, logistics, communications, and policy decisions beyond the software's scope.

A browser-based reporting channel must complement rather than displace telephone, radio, paper, and in-person procedures. Otherwise, communities in areas with weak connectivity, limited device access, or disrupted power may become less visible instead of better represented. The system's contribution to affected communities should therefore be evaluated through the completeness and traceability of local information available to authorized decision-makers, not through unmeasured claims about response speed, casualty reduction, or relief outcomes.

Disaggregated information may help authorized personnel recognize needs that totals conceal, but collecting granular location, disability, health, displacement, age, sex, or other vulnerability information can also increase re-identification, stigma, surveillance, and security risks. The platform should collect only decision-relevant data under a documented lawful purpose, restrict detailed access, distinguish zero from unknown or no report, and prefer aggregate or de-identified disclosure where identity is unnecessary. Differences in reporting volume must not automatically be interpreted as differences in disaster impact because device access, connectivity, language, trust, and staffing can make some communities less visible in digital records.

### System Administrators and Data-Protection Officers

The project provides technical support for managing user accounts, permissions, sessions, event logs, backups, record retention, and controlled exports. This is important because disaster records may contain personal, sensitive, and operational information. A designated Data Protection Officer, Privacy Impact Assessment, processing inventory, privacy management program, breach response, business-continuity plan, and staff training are organizational controls that the prototype can support but cannot satisfy on its own (National Privacy Commission, 2016; National Privacy Commission, 2023).

In a multi-organization deployment, these responsibilities must be divided clearly. Under an outsourcing arrangement in which the DRRM organization determines the purpose and essential scope of processing, the organization ordinarily acts as personal information controller and the startup as personal information processor. The final legal roles depend on the actual arrangement, especially if the startup reuses customer information for an independent purpose. Each organization needs a responsible local administrator, data owner, records officer, and privacy authority, while the startup needs accountable security, support, and service owners. Support access should be exceptional, authorized, time-bound, limited in scope, logged, and revoked when the support task ends.

### Future Researchers and System Developers

The study can provide a documented case of translating local reporting workflows into role rules, organization and jurisdiction boundaries, verification states, data definitions, SitRep mappings, configuration versions, and measurable acceptance criteria. Future work may examine adoption, longitudinal data quality, interoperability, offline continuity, native applications, SMS fallback, geospatial analysis, additional hazards, or other organization types. Historical data must be reused only under appropriate authorization, privacy, retention, and ethical controls; the existence of a database does not automatically make personal disaster records available for research.

A multi-organization pilot can also show which parts of the reporting process remain stable across participating DRRM organizations and which require local configuration. Results should be analyzed by organization type, jurisdiction, user role, hazard scenario, connectivity condition, workflow version, and device. Similar results in additional sites can strengthen transferability, but a convenience sample of willing organizations cannot establish national representativeness. Future researchers should therefore preserve the context of each result instead of reporting one combined average as evidence for all Philippine DRRM offices.

## 1.5.2 Operational, Technical, Economic, and Governance Significance

### Operational Significance

The system preserves each organization's chain of responsibility while making handoffs and report status explicit. Its operational contribution is the replacement of repeated, unstructured encoding with shared data fields, validation, verification states, amendment history, and controlled consolidation. A fair evaluation should compare the existing and proposed processes using equivalent incident scenarios and measure elapsed time, omissions, duplicate records, corrections, number of handoffs, queue delay, and retrieval success. Any improvement should be reported as an observed result with its participants, tasks, devices, workflow configuration, and network conditions, not generalized beyond the evaluation context.

Operational significance also depends on correct configuration and continuing support. Roles, jurisdiction assignments, approval routes, and report templates must reflect actual local procedures. The startup must maintain monitoring, incident response, updates, backups, restoration, and help-desk support, while each customer must retain trained users, administrators, approval authorities, and tested non-web continuity procedures. Adoption should be measured through sustained role-appropriate use during realistic tasks rather than account creation or training attendance alone.

### Technical Significance

Using one responsive web application avoids the immediate complexity of distributing and maintaining separate native mobile and desktop clients. The same role-controlled workflow can be accessed through supported desktop and mobile browsers, while responsive layouts adapt content to different widths and input methods. Technical feasibility must nevertheless be demonstrated through browser and viewport compatibility, touch and keyboard use, readable reflow, validation behavior, performance, security testing, backup restoration, and behavior during interrupted requests. WCAG 2.2 supplies applicable accessibility criteria across responsive variations of a page (W3C, 2023; ICTO-NCDA, 2010).

One responsive codebase also supports controlled reuse across customers, but centralization concentrates technical dependency. A platform outage, privileged-account compromise, configuration error, or tenant-isolation failure may affect more than one organization. Organization identifiers and access boundaries must therefore be enforced at the server and data-access layers and verified through cross-organization tests. Customer configurations and template versions should be preserved with historical records so that later changes do not alter the meaning of previously approved reports.

The responsive approach does not provide native-device capabilities or guaranteed continuity during loss of connectivity. Internet availability is an implementation assumption. Mobile-browser evaluation should include realistic bandwidth, interruption, restoration, orientation, touch, and software-keyboard conditions rather than desktop resizing alone. Section 19 of the E-Governance Act also requires government to consider alternate delivery modes for people without internet access. This does not require the capstone to implement SMS or offline operation, but it confirms that existing non-web procedures remain necessary during outages or for excluded users (Republic Act No. 12254, 2025).

### Economic Significance

The web approach may reduce duplicated development and maintenance effort because the startup maintains one application codebase and participating organizations can use existing browser-capable devices. Centralized records may also reduce repeated encoding, follow-up communication, manual consolidation, and document-retrieval effort. These are plausible resource benefits rather than proven savings. Economic evaluation should record development and hosting costs, training time, support effort, report-processing time, repeated-entry effort, and any avoided printing or storage cost. A monetary return on investment should be claimed only if both costs and realized benefits are measured over a defined period.

For a shared startup service, total cost of ownership should also include organization setup, configuration, subscription or service fees, connectivity, procurement and contract management, security review, technical support, data export, and migration. Shared hosting and maintenance may create economies of scale, but they also create risks of limited customization, provider dependency, and costly exit. World Bank GovTech procurement guidance treats software-as-a-service, commercial software, and custom development as context-dependent acquisition choices rather than assuming that one model is always less expensive (World Bank, 2021).

Because prospective customers are public institutions, commercialization also depends on government procurement and continuing performance evaluation. A recurring service arrangement does not exempt the organization from procurement planning, appropriations, competition or another legally available modality, contract administration, and value-for-money review. The project's economic significance is therefore a proposition to be assessed, not proof that the startup model is already commercially feasible (Republic Act No. 12009, 2024).

### Governance and Accountability Significance

The system can make data provenance and institutional decisions more visible through recorded submitters, timestamps, validation results, reasons for return or rejection, amendments, report versions, and audit logs. Authentic and usable public records also require controlled versioning, metadata, protection, scheduled retention, legal or archival holds, authorized transfer, and National Archives-approved disposition. A database record is not automatically public or freely reusable: operational access, public disclosure, and research reuse have different lawful bases and safeguards, and aggregate or de-identified output should be preferred when identity is unnecessary (Republic Act No. 9470, 2007; National Privacy Commission, 2016).

The multi-organization model requires a clear separation between platform service administration and statutory authority. The startup may provision customer accounts, maintain hosting, monitor availability, deploy updates, and provide authorized support, but each DRRM organization remains responsible for defining reporting purposes, assigning local roles, validating incidents, approving SitReps, determining disclosure, and governing public records. A service agreement with the startup does not authorize one customer to access another customer's data. Any later inter-organization sharing requires its own lawful and operational basis, safeguards, and accountable approval.

Stable identifiers, geographic units, hazard categories, statuses, timestamps, versioned data dictionaries, and documented interfaces can prepare records for more consistent future exchange. They do not by themselves establish semantic or organizational interoperability or authorize external disclosure. If adopted as an LGU system, the design aligns with the E-Governance Act's direction on interoperable government processes, records management, risk-based security, and privacy engineering, but institutional adoption, DICT alignment, policy, operation, and testing remain separate requirements (Republic Act No. 12254, 2025).

Customer significance also depends on a workable exit. Contracts and system functions should define customer custody, export formats, included records and metadata, versions and audit evidence, retention and legal holds, assistance, access revocation, and the treatment of processor-held copies. Portability is demonstrated only when an organization's records and metadata can be exported completely in a documented, usable form. A download button alone does not establish that the records can be independently understood or migrated.

The Sendai Framework emphasizes the availability and accessibility of disaster-risk information as part of understanding disaster risk. The project contributes narrowly to that goal by improving how authorized local actors structure and retrieve operational records; it is not itself a complete early-warning, public-information, or disaster-response system (UNDRR, 2015).

Electronic records may also preserve approval identity, role, time, report version, and integrity evidence more consistently than an untracked file exchange. The Electronic Commerce Act recognizes government use of electronic documents and signatures, but acceptance still depends on the responsible agency's prescribed requirements and the reliability of the method used. The platform can therefore support authentic and attributable approval evidence without claiming that every generated file is automatically an official, admissible, or accepted SitRep (Republic Act No. 8792, 2000).

## 1.5.3 Conditions for Realizing the Benefits

The expected benefits are conditional on representative user participation, accurate source data, timely verification, reliable hosting and connectivity, role and privacy policies, training, technical support, and continued administrative ownership. Evaluation should report the context of use: participant roles and experience, organization type, jurisdiction, task scenarios, workflow and template versions, devices, browsers, network conditions, sample size, assistance allowed, and the exact operational definitions of each metric. This distinction between implemented capability, measured result, anticipated benefit, limitation, and future work keeps the project's significance informative without overstating what the prototype can establish.

For the startup-operated model, realizing benefits also requires a named service owner, local organization administrator, data owner, records officer, privacy authority, procurement authority, and personnel responsible for continuity. The organization and startup need documented responsibilities for configuration, monitoring, support, security incidents, backups, restoration, access requests, exports, and offboarding. Measures should be separated by organization and role because a combined average may conceal a reporting burden or usability problem experienced by a smaller group.

Standardization should leave controlled room for information that predefined categories cannot anticipate. Narrative remarks, attachments where authorized, and explicit unknown or not-yet-verified states can preserve operationally important context while structured fields support comparison. Organizations must maintain training and support, review whether fields remain aligned with actual workflows, and use the records in decision processes; a technically functional database alone cannot produce these benefits.

Evaluation should also examine unintended outcomes. These include duplicate reporting, assignment to the wrong organization or jurisdiction, excessive notifications, approval bottlenecks, added encoding work, automation bias, inappropriate platform-support access, and exclusion caused by connectivity or accessibility barriers. Reporting these effects is necessary to determine whether the platform fits its operating environment rather than merely demonstrating that its planned features function.
