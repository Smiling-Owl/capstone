## 1.1 Project Context

The Philippine disaster risk reduction and management framework requires coordinated action across national and local institutions. Republic Act No. 10121 established a Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office in every province, city, and municipality and a Barangay Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Committee (BDRRMC) in every barangay. These local bodies are responsible for monitoring hazards, consolidating risk information, maintaining local databases, coordinating response activities, and communicating disaster information within their jurisdictions (Republic Act No. 10121, 2010, secs. 11–12).

At the barangay level, the current *Listo si KAP* framework distinguishes the policy and approval role of the Barangay Development Council from the implementation role of the BDRRMC. Barangay institutions are expected to prepare and carry out local disaster measures, maintain readiness, activate local operations, manage evacuation sites, coordinate rescue activities, and assist in damage assessment and recovery. The city government provides coordination and augmentation when an incident exceeds barangay capacity or affects a wider jurisdiction (Department of the Interior and Local Government, 2025).

In Zamboanga City, the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (ZCDRRMO) performs these responsibilities through its Administration and Training, Research and Planning, and Operations and Warning functions. Its official responsibilities include consolidating disaster-risk information, maintaining risk and resource records, supporting preparedness planning, monitoring hazards, issuing warnings, coordinating responders and partner organizations, and supporting the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council. The office also uses several communication channels, including radio, landline, and other communication technologies, to reach communities and coordinate operations (City Government of Zamboanga, n.d.).

This local structure operates in a multi-hazard environment. Recent government reports document recurring flooding in vulnerable Zamboanga City communities. The city's adoption of the Rapid Earthquake Damage Assessment System also supports local planning for hazards such as earthquakes, tsunamis, floods, storm surges, and rain-induced landslides (Department of Science and Technology, 2025). The progressive DSWD reporting series for the June 2025 flooding in Zamboanga City further demonstrates that disaster information changes as new observations and assessments become available (Department of Social Welfare and Development, 2025).

Consequently, local disaster reporting is not a single act of preparing a document. Information begins with field or community observations, passes through barangay and city personnel, undergoes clarification and verification, and is consolidated into successive reports for authorized recipients. The 2024 NDRRM Operations Center guidelines distinguish initial, progress, terminal, cumulative situation, and final reports and require later updates to remain supported by identifiable sources (National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council [NDRRMC], 2024, pp. 45, 52–55). A local information system must therefore preserve the source, place, observation and submission times, verification status, corrections, and relationship between early records and later consolidated outputs.

Interviews conducted for this study indicate that the Zamboanga City workflow still depends on follow-up calls, information received through different channels, manual consolidation, and personnel-intensive verification. Records also originate from several office functions, while access to operational and personal information must remain controlled. These conditions create a need for a role-based system that provides a shared and traceable incident record without replacing authorized assessment, approval, emergency communication, or official government reporting procedures.

The proposed system will support structured incident submission, controlled information sharing, human-led verification, version preservation, searchable records, and the preparation of reviewable Situation Report drafts. It will serve as a connected system of record for authorized barangay and ZCDRRMO users. Existing radio, telephone, paper, and other contingency procedures must remain available during power, internet, telecommunications, or hosting disruptions.

## 1.2 Statement of the Problem and Purpose

The current disaster-reporting workflow described by the interviewed ZCDRRMO practitioner contains four related problems: communication delay, manual record-management delay, limited data sharing, and inefficient verification. These problems occur at different handoffs between barangay personnel, operational units, assessors, documentation personnel, and approving authorities. They should therefore be discussed separately rather than treated as one general problem.

### A. Communication Delay

Incident information does not always move directly from the barangay to the city disaster office. The interviewed practitioner explained:

> “Halimbawa yung barangay nilagay mo diyan gagawa sila ng report, kami pa yan ang tatawag minsan ... nagpapadala pa yan kami ng tao, ma'am to verify. ... Ang nangyayari, kahit sa submission ng report, we had to call them.”

> *For example, when a barangay is expected to prepare a report, we still have to call them and may send personnel to verify. Even for report submission, we have to call them.* (ZCDRRMO key-informant interview, 36:27–38:21)

This creates an additional handoff between local observation, barangay reporting, city follow-up, and consolidation. Until the source responds or supporting information is obtained, the city office may lack a sufficiently documented record for its evolving situational picture. National guidance similarly requires information gathered from originating councils and agencies to undergo verification and validation before official dissemination (NDRRMC, 2024, p. 45). The problem is therefore not the use of communication channels themselves, but the absence of a shared and visible submission, acknowledgment, and follow-up status.

### B. Manual Record-Management Delay

The reporting burden continues after information is received. When asked whether reports still require manual editing, the interviewee answered:

> “Yes, kaya siya ganon kasimple lang. Pero kung may way na automatic yung generation, maganda sana. ... Sinubmit ko yung full, hiningi niya ito, pagkatapos nito, pinasummarize niya pa sa akin.”

> *Yes, that is why it is kept simple. If there were a way to automate the preparation, that would be useful. I submitted the full version, and afterward I was asked to summarize it again.* (ZCDRRMO key-informant interview, 29:28–29:44; 30:02–31:35)

The workflow moves from collected incident information to manual consolidation, recipient-specific editing, review, and sometimes further condensation. Repeated handling consumes staff time and can produce omissions or inconsistent copies whose relationship is difficult to trace. Because disaster information changes across initial, progress, terminal, cumulative situation, and final reports, the required response is not merely one-click document generation. The system must preserve reusable incident data, provenance, status, corrections, and version history while allowing authorized personnel to prepare and review outputs for different recipients (NDRRMC, 2024, pp. 52–55).

### C. Limited Data Sharing

The interview also revealed a knowledge- and record-sharing gap among office functions. The practitioner stated:

> “Ibig sabihin, lahat ng alam ko should be shared. ... Unfortunately dito sa office natin ... ang research and planning division, they don't know ... what we do dito sa training ... operation ... [and] EMS. That's why they cannot effectively come up with situational report.”

> *Knowledge should be shared. However, research and planning does not always know what training, operations, and EMS do, which affects its ability to prepare the report effectively.* (ZCDRRMO key-informant interview, 1:03:44–1:06:54)

Information from operational divisions may therefore pass to documentation personnel and return for clarification or correction. This circular handoff can delay consolidation and introduce inconsistent terminology or role descriptions. The remedy, however, is not unrestricted access. The same participant explained that access remains limited even within the office, and disaster records may contain names, health information, casualty details, and other protected data. The proposed system should consequently provide a shared but role-governed incident record with source attribution, controlled views, and access history. This supports the local information-management functions established by RA 10121 while respecting the proportionality, confidentiality, integrity, and accountability requirements of the Data Privacy Act and government data-security rules (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012; National Privacy Commission, 2016).

### D. Inefficient Verification

Verification remains necessary, but the current routing described in the interview can be resource-intensive. The practitioner explained:

> “Most often than not, we even have to send people to verify which is not effective. ... Pero dahil sa kulang ang tao, iilan lang din ang capable ... apart from ako yung team lead, ako yung lead sa extrication, sa medical services, ako din naglead ng assessment.”

> *More often than not, we send people to verify. Because personnel are insufficient and only a few are capable, the same person may lead the team, extrication, medical services, and assessment.* (ZCDRRMO key-informant interview, 36:27–38:21; 43:02–45:13)

When information is incomplete, indirect, or unsupported, city personnel may call its source or dispatch qualified staff for field assessment. The sequence of receipt, follow-up, dispatch, assessment, documentation, and approval can compete with operational duties. Field verification cannot simply be removed because official reporting requires validation and source documentation (NDRRMC, 2024, pp. 45, 52–55). The bounded problem is the inefficient routing and documentation of verification work. The proposed system should make the source, evidence, assigned reviewer, status, requests for correction, decision, rationale, and timestamps visible while retaining qualified human judgment.

### Purpose of the Study

This study aims to design and evaluate a role-based disaster incident reporting and record management system for authorized barangay and ZCDRRMO users. The system will support time-stamped submissions, structured incident records, evidence attachment, controlled inter-unit sharing, status-based human verification, correction history, reusable summaries, and traceable Situation Report drafting. Its purpose is to reduce avoidable follow-up and duplicate encoding while preserving source provenance, privacy controls, official review and approval, and existing government reporting procedures.

The study will not assume that a submitted digital record is automatically accurate or verified. It will not replace field assessment, incident command, emergency dispatch, official signatures, mayoral or office approval, public-warning procedures, or submission requirements imposed by OCD, NDRRMC, DILG, or other authorized agencies.

## 1.3 Objectives

### General Objective

To design, develop, and evaluate a role-based system that supports the submission, verification, consolidation, retrieval, and monitoring of traceable disaster incident records for barangay and ZCDRRMO users.

### Specific Objectives

1. Develop user and role management that restricts records and actions to authorized barangay and ZCDRRMO personnel.
2. Develop structured incident reporting with source, location, time, impact, needs, evidence, and submission-status fields.
3. Develop human-led verification with reviewer assignments, clarification requests, evidence review, decisions, reasons, and correction history.
4. Develop controlled Situation Report drafting from eligible incident records while retaining links to their sources and verification status.
5. Develop a searchable catalog that preserves incident records, report versions, access history, and authorized exports.
6. Develop role-based dashboards that display report status, coverage, processing time, and provisional and verified values.
7. Evaluate the system through functional, role-access, traceability, usability, and timed workflow tests using declared scenarios and users.

## Evidence Boundaries

- The interview reflects one key informant's experience and should not be presented as representative of every Zamboanga City barangay or every ZCDRRMO division.
- The quoted estimate that follow-up or dispatch occurs “90% of the time or more” was not independently verified and is not repeated as a factual rate in the revised prose.
- Official sources establish reporting, coordination, verification, privacy, and record-management responsibilities but do not prove that the proposed system will reduce response time or improve public-safety outcomes.
- Measurable claims about reduced preparation or verification time require baseline and post-implementation testing.
