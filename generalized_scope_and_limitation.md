# 1.4 Scope and Limitations

This study covers the design, development, and prototype evaluation of a centralized, role-based, responsive Online Disaster Situation Report Generation and Record Management System operated by a startup for participating disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) organizations. The platform is conceived as a configurable service rather than a system permanently owned and administered by ZCDRRMO alone. ZCDRRMO may serve as an original study setting, pilot organization, or customer, but a participating provincial, city, municipal, barangay, or other authorized DRRM organization is established as a separate organizational environment and retains authority over its jurisdiction, users, reporting route, verification and approval decisions, records, disclosures, and official submissions. The startup operates the technical service and does not acquire the statutory powers of an LDRRMO, local government, Incident Command System authority, or national agency under Republic Act No. 10121.

The system includes the **User and Organization Management Module**, **Disaster Reporting Module**, **Disaster Report Verification Module**, **SitRep Generation Module**, **SitRep Catalog Module**, and **Dashboard Module**. The prototype covers local reporting for floods, typhoons, and fires. Its controlled terms and fields may be configured for participating organizations, but support for other hazards requires separate requirements analysis, domain validation, test data, and approval before it can be claimed. The service supports customer administrators, reporters, reviewers or verifiers, approvers, authorized viewers, records and privacy personnel, and limited startup support personnel. Actual role titles and escalation routes may differ among organizations and must be mapped during onboarding rather than assumed to be identical nationwide.

The User and Organization Management Module covers organization onboarding and basic configuration; account creation or registration; authentication; activation, suspension, and deactivation; jurisdiction membership; role assignment; administrator handover; and access-event recording. Organization identity and jurisdiction must constrain server-side access to records, searches, attachments, dashboards, background actions, exports, and audit entries. A startup account does not receive standing access to all customer records. When technical access is necessary, it must be authorized by the organization, limited by role, record scope, purpose, and duration, logged, reviewable, and revoked when the support activity ends. Account removal will not erase the authorship or approval history of public records.

The Disaster Reporting Module covers draft, submitted, corrected, and reconciled incident information. Records may include stable identifiers; hazard and incident description; organization and jurisdiction; location; occurrence, observation, receipt, and submission times; source channel; affected population; casualties; damage to infrastructure or property; immediate needs; actions taken; attachments where authorized; and an explicit indication that a value is unknown, provisional, disputed, or not applicable. Initial reports may legitimately be incomplete because the 2024 NDRRM Operations Center guidelines recognize progressive reporting as information develops. The system therefore supports structured completeness without converting unavailable information into zero or presenting an early estimate as settled fact. Automated validation is limited to syntactic, domain, range, consistency, and possible-duplicate checks and does not establish factual accuracy.

The Disaster Report Verification Module covers triage, review, clarification, correction, corroboration, validation, partial validation where configured, return, rejection, and reconciliation. It records who made each decision, under which organization and role, when it was made, what source version was examined, and why information was returned, accepted, or rejected. Duplicate and conflict flags assist reviewers but do not automatically merge records or decide which source is authoritative. Late reports and corrections remain linked to the affected record and reporting period. Material changes create a new version or supersession relationship rather than replacing the prior submitted or approved account without history.

The SitRep Generation Module selects eligible verified information at a declared cut-off time and produces a reviewable local report. It may map data to applicable NDRRM Operations Center categories, including the prevailing situation, preparedness measures, response actions, consolidated effects, issues and concerns, and recommendations. The report can identify its stage, operational or reporting period, source coverage, unresolved discrepancies, generation time, template version, approval status, and superseded output where applicable. It preserves links from consolidated assertions and totals to their source report versions and verification decisions. The module assists authorized SitRep developers and approvers; it does not replace Incident Command System responsibilities, issue a national NDRRMC SitRep, establish legal truth, or automatically transmit an approved report.

The SitRep Catalog Module covers both historical reports and the controlled reference values needed to interpret them. Authorized users may search and filter records; view source, status, version, and approval information; compare or supersede versions; and export permitted information. Controlled hazards, places, report types, units, statuses, and data definitions are deactivated or versioned instead of destructively changed where historical reports depend on them. Records may be assigned a classification, retention trigger, disposition status, and legal or archival hold. The customer organization remains responsible for its public-record custody, approved retention schedule, archival transfer, and authorized disposition under Republic Act No. 9470; termination of a subscription does not authorize the startup to destroy government records.

The Dashboard Module covers operational summaries derived from records the authenticated user is permitted to access. It may display incident and report counts, affected-population figures, verification queues and outcomes, SitRep coverage, processing time, and data freshness. Provisional and verified figures are shown separately where both are used. Filters, units, reporting period, last update, missingness, and underlying permitted records remain visible so that users can interpret a total in context. Public-facing disclosure is not included merely because a dashboard exists. Any later public view requires separate approval, minimization, aggregation or suppression, and testing against re-identification through small counts, precise locations, or combined attributes.

The application is delivered through supported desktop, tablet, and smartphone web browsers. In connected but degraded conditions, the prototype may show explicit draft and submission states, retain a minimum necessary draft where safe, use a stable request identifier, and support controlled retry or reconciliation after an interrupted request. The exact behavior will be tested for loss before transmission, loss after server receipt but before acknowledgement, attachment interruption, session expiry, refresh, retry, and concurrent correction. Browser draft preservation is not the same as offline synchronization and may be restricted on shared devices or for sensitive information. A successful retry cannot recover data that was neither safely retained nor received by the server.

Accessibility is included as an evaluation and design requirement for implemented end-to-end tasks. Testing may cover semantic relationships, labeled controls, keyboard operation, focus order and visibility, error identification, non-color cues, target size, accessible authentication, zoom and reflow, status messages, tabular alternatives to charts, and selected screen-reader behavior using declared devices, browsers, and assistive technology in reference to WCAG 2.2. The study will report the pages, tasks, criteria, methods, and unresolved failures actually tested. It will not claim universal accessibility, formal certification, accurate support for untested languages, or coverage of every disability based only on automated scans.

The evaluation covers functional, integration, organization-isolation, role-authorization, data-quality, workflow, SitRep, accessibility, recovery, export, and role-based user scenarios. A prepared dataset will contain valid, incomplete, conflicting, duplicate, late, corrected, rejected, superseded, and wrong-organization records with expected results. Where feasible, equivalent tasks will be observed in the current paper, spreadsheet, messaging, or email workflow. The study can compare task completion, errors, elapsed time, field completeness, corrections, verification turnaround, aggregation and retrieval accuracy, source traceability, access denials, restoration, and export. It may also record usability, intention to use, administrator workload, support requests, training needs, and indicative lifecycle costs. These measures evaluate separate properties; a favorable satisfaction score cannot substitute for accuracy, accessibility, security, adoption, or willingness to pay.

The prototype remains a connected-web system. It does not include a native mobile application, full offline synchronization, SMS gateway, radio integration, satellite or mesh networking, custom telecommunications hardware, or guaranteed operation without electricity or connectivity. Participating organizations must retain approved radio, telephone, paper, face-to-face, or other contingency procedures during outages. When information from an alternate channel is later encoded, the organization must record its original source, event and receipt times, encoder, and duplicate or conflict resolution so that later entry does not appear to be a real-time web submission. Republic Act No. 12234 and Republic Act No. 12254 support improved connectivity and resilient digital government, but neither law establishes continuous access in every target community.

The system does not autonomously verify whether an incident is true; command an emergency response; declare a state of calamity; allocate funds, relief, or personnel; issue evacuation orders; dispatch responders; forecast hazards; conduct GIS risk modelling; operate sensors; or publish official public warnings. It does not replace PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, NDRRMC, OCD, DILG procedures, emergency hotlines, Incident Command System forms, or authorized public-information channels. It excludes predictive artificial intelligence and automated government decision-making. Structured reports and dashboards may make the recorded information easier to examine, but incomplete coverage, delayed encoding, biased reporting, or erroneous sources may still produce a misleading picture.

The prototype has no live integration with NDRRMC, OCD, DILG, DSWD, PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, GeoRiskPH, PhilSys, or another external government system unless an interface is later named, implemented, authorized, and tested. Stable identifiers, controlled vocabularies, geographic codes, timestamps, units, schemas, and documented exports may improve readiness for later exchange; they do not establish technical, semantic, organizational, or legal interoperability. The Common Alerting Protocol may be relevant to a future authorized public-warning exchange but is not a complete SitRep schema. Any future API, identity federation, electronic-signature service, or inter-agency exchange requires the receiving authority's specifications, agreements, authentication, security review, and correction procedure.

Disaster records may contain personal, sensitive personal, operational, or security-sensitive information such as names, contact information, health or casualty details, disability information, precise locations, images, household records, and information concerning children or displaced persons. Collection and access must be connected to a documented lawful purpose and limited to necessary information. Under an outsourced service arrangement, the participating organization ordinarily determines the public purpose and authorized disclosure, while the startup processes information under documented instructions and safeguards; exact roles depend on the operation and agreement. Software controls cannot by themselves establish compliance with Republic Act No. 10173. Compliance also depends on privacy notices, a Privacy Impact Assessment where required, processing agreements, trained personnel, breach response, approved retention, support controls, and actual practice.

The study includes only preliminary feasibility evidence for the startup model. It may document onboarding effort, training and support requirements, a hosting assumption, backup and export demonstrations, potential pricing bases, and indicative acquisition, configuration, migration, hosting, storage, maintenance, security, support, and exit costs. It does not establish PhilGEPS or bidder eligibility, a procurement award, production service levels, cybersecurity certification, profitability, scalable market demand, willingness to pay, or return on investment. Republic Act No. 12009 provides a lifecycle and market-scoping context, but the procuring entity—not the startup or the prototype study—selects and justifies a lawful procurement method.

Finally, the study cannot by itself prove reduced casualties, faster rescue, more equitable assistance, improved resource allocation, stronger resilience, or compliance by participating organizations. Those outcomes depend on source-data quality, staffing, training, command and approval arrangements, connectivity, logistics, public trust, resources, and actual operational use. Findings apply only to the participating organizations, roles, hazards, scenarios, languages, devices, accessibility configurations, dataset, network conditions, and study period. Results from one organization or a convenience sample will not be generalized to all Philippine DRRM organizations, and absence of a digital report will not be treated as evidence that a locality or population was unaffected.
