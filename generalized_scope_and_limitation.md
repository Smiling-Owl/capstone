# 1.4 Scope and Limitations

This study covers the design, development, and prototype evaluation of a centralized, role-based, responsive Online Disaster Situation Report Generation and Record Management System operated by a startup for participating disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) organizations. Unlike the earlier organization-specific concept, the platform is not owned or administered exclusively by ZCDRRMO. A participating provincial, city, municipal, barangay, or other authorized DRRM organization may be configured as a separate customer environment while retaining authority over its jurisdiction, users, reporting workflow, records, verification decisions, approved SitReps, disclosures, and official submissions. The startup is responsible for operating and maintaining the technical service but does not assume the statutory powers of a DRRM office.

The system includes the **User and Organization Management Module**, **Disaster Reporting Module**, **Disaster Report Verification Module**, **SitRep Generation Module**, **SitRep Catalog Module**, and **Dashboard Module**. The prototype covers floods, typhoons, and fires. Each participating organization may assign authorized administrators, reporters, reviewers, verifiers, approvers, and viewers according to its local structure. Role and organization boundaries govern which records and actions are available to each account; startup personnel may configure organizations and provide authorized technical support but may not independently validate incidents or approve official reports.

The User and Organization Management Module covers organization onboarding and configuration, account registration or creation, account verification, authentication, account status, jurisdiction assignment, and role-based access. Each organization's identity and jurisdiction are applied to server-side record access, searches, dashboards, files, approvals, exports, and audit entries. Any startup support access to customer records must be specifically authorized, limited in purpose and duration, logged, and removed when the support activity ends.

The Disaster Reporting Module allows authorized reporters to submit and update structured initial and detailed incident information. Its data may include the incident type, organization, jurisdiction, location, incident and submission times, affected population, casualties, damage, immediate needs, actions taken, source, attachments where authorized, and explicit unknown or not-yet-verified values. Automated checks may identify missing, invalid, inconsistent, or potentially duplicate entries, but authorized personnel remain responsible for confirming whether field observations are accurate.

The Disaster Report Verification Module supports review, return for correction, validation, and rejection with a recorded reason. It preserves the report's source, status, responsible actor, timestamps, and material version history. The SitRep Generation Module retrieves only eligible validated records within the authorized organization and jurisdiction, consolidates applicable barangay or local data, and maps the information to relevant NDRRM Operations Center SitRep categories. A generated report remains a reviewable local output until the responsible organization approves and submits it through its authorized procedure (NDRRMC, 2024).

The SitRep Catalog Module stores reports chronologically and provides authorized viewing, search, filtering, export, and amendment while preserving prior approved versions and source relationships. The Dashboard Module summarizes organization-scoped incident counts, affected-population data, report status, SitRep activity, and other implemented indicators. The system also maintains logs for security-relevant and record-changing actions and supports backups, restoration, and an organization-controlled export of records, metadata, versions, and authorized attachments. Public records hosted by the startup remain subject to the responsible government office's custody, retention, archival, and disposition authority under Republic Act No. 9470.

The application is accessed through supported desktop, tablet, and smartphone web browsers and does not require a separately installed mobile application. Its evaluation covers the implemented modules through functional, integration, security, accessibility, recovery, and role-based user tests. Testing may examine task completion, errors, report completeness and validity, verification turnaround, aggregation accuracy, source traceability, retrieval and dashboard accuracy, organization isolation, export completeness, restoration, and user feedback. Findings apply only to the declared prototype version, participants, data, devices, browsers, scenarios, network conditions, and test period.

The study is limited to connected web operation. It does not include native mobile applications, offline synchronization, SMS fallback, radio integration, or custom telecommunications hardware. Use therefore depends on a compatible device, electricity, internet connectivity, hosting availability, and functioning telecommunications. Participating organizations must retain their approved radio, telephone, paper, face-to-face, or other contingency procedures during outages and reconcile resulting records when connectivity returns.

The system does not autonomously verify the truth of a report, approve a SitRep, declare a state of calamity, allocate funds or relief, issue evacuation orders, dispatch responders, forecast hazards, or send public warnings. It excludes predictive analytics, artificial-intelligence decision-making, meteorological modelling, sensor networks, and direct control of emergency communication channels. Source information may remain late, incomplete, inaccurate, biased, duplicated, or false despite the use of structured forms and validation rules.

The prototype has no implemented live integration with NDRRMC, OCD, DILG, DSWD, PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, GeoRiskPH, or other external government systems. Structured fields, identifiers, templates, and exports may support later interoperability, but future exchange would require the receiving agency's specifications, authority, security review, testing, and agreements. Electronic records and approval evidence likewise remain subject to the responsible agency's prescribed procedure under Republic Act No. 8792.

Disaster records may contain personal, sensitive personal, operational, or security-sensitive information. Compliance with Republic Act No. 10173 depends not only on software controls but also on lawful purpose, proportional collection, privacy notices, a Privacy Impact Assessment where required, trained personnel, processing agreements, incident response, retention rules, and actual organizational practice. The study does not establish production-grade availability, resistance to every attack, national scalability, sustained adoption, procurement eligibility, commercial viability, or return on investment. These would require further deployment evidence, contracts and service levels, procurement review, operational funding, security assessment, continuity arrangements, training, support, and a tested provider-exit process.

Finally, prototype testing cannot by itself prove reduced casualties, faster rescue, equitable relief distribution, improved policy decisions, or compliance by participating organizations. Such outcomes also depend on staffing, training, source-data quality, connectivity, logistics, trust, institutional authority, and continued use. Results from one pilot organization or a limited participant sample will not be generalized to all Philippine DRRM organizations without further evidence.
