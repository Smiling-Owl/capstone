IMPLICATIONS OF THE CURRENT WORKFLOW
Repeated follow-up and re-entry affect the reporting chain
Timeliness
Calls and clarification requests add hand-offs before staff can consolidate a sufficiently documented incident picture.
Staff effort
The same information may be edited, summarized and re-entered for different recipients.
Record continuity
Separate messages and files make it harder to identify the current report version and its source.
Verification load
Personnel may need repeated follow-up or field checks while covering several operational duties.
Controlled sharing
Information needs to reach report preparers, but access cannot be unrestricted because records may contain protected details.
Source: revised paper, Statement of the Problem and ZCDRRMO interview

POSSIBLE SOLUTIONS AND WHY NOT
Useful tools are insufficient when used alone
Approach	What it supports	Why it is not sufficient alone
Calls, SMS, radio, messaging	Fast contact and urgent coordination	Status, source, correction and version history remain spread across channels
Paper forms and spreadsheets	Flexible local capture and manual review	Copies require manual reconciliation and repeated summary work
Single-level reporting app	Structured electronic submission	May not represent Purok to Barangay to DRRM review and cumulative SitRep history
Proposed integrated web system	Role-based reporting, verification, history and SitRep preparation	Still depends on connectivity and central service, so contingency channels remain necessary

PROBLEM STATEMENT
The paper identifies four distinct operational problems
01
Communication delay
Submission, acknowledgement and clarification depend on follow-up.
02
Manual record management
Staff combine records and may summarize the same information again.
03
Limited data sharing
Separate office functions do not always share the information needed for SitRep preparation.
04
Inefficient verification
Incomplete or inconsistent details prompt additional calls or field checks.
The paper describes an information-governance problem, not a lack of communication channels alone.

PROPOSED SYSTEM
Role-based system for DRRM reporting
WHAT IT IS
A centralized web system for aggregate profiles, incident reports, verification, report history and editable SitRep drafts.
WHY IT IS NEEDED
The paper identifies delays, manual record handling, limited data sharing and inefficient verification in the current workflow.
HOW IT WORKS
Purok submits initial information. Barangay verifies and consolidates it. DRRM reviews verified reports and prepares the SitRep draft.
Verified records retain their source and version. Corrections return to the responsible level.
Human officials retain authority over factual review, approval and official release.
Source: revised paper, General Objective, Scope and Limitations

PROPOSED SYSTEM MODULES: DETAILED
Purok accounts and profile modules
MODULE
HOW IT WORKS
WHY IT EXISTS
Purok User Management
Authorized Barangay personnel create a Purok account, assign its Barangay and issue credentials.
Correct jurisdiction routes submissions to the responsible reviewer.
Purok Profile Creation
Record Purok identity and monthly aggregate population, housing, vulnerability and hazard exposure values.
Establishes the baseline for exposure analysis and Barangay consolidation. No named household records are required.
Purok Profile Verification
A Barangay reviewer compares the submitted profile with reference information and approves or returns it with a reason.
Keeps unverified baseline data from being treated as confirmed.
Source: revised paper, Specific Objectives 303–305 and Table 3

SLIDE 21
========================================================================
PROPOSED SYSTEM MODULES: DETAILED
Purok incident reporting modules
MODULE
HOW IT WORKS
WHY IT EXISTS
Purok Incident Reporting
Submit hazard, location, observed date and time, initial situation and affected or exposure-unit totals. Keep later changes as versions.
Captures a useful first report without making Purok complete the full SitRep assessment.
Purok Incident Report Verification
Barangay reviewers compare sources and versions, flag possible duplicates or conflicts, then validate, partially validate, return or reject with a reason.
Makes review and correction accountable. Automated checks do not decide factual accuracy.
Purok Incident Report History
Retrieve current and prior versions of Purok incident reports with their status.
Shows what changed as the event developed and preserves the originating record.
Source: revised paper, Specific Objectives 306–308 and Table 3

PROPOSED SYSTEM MODULES: DETAILED
Barangay accounts and profile modules
MODULE
HOW IT WORKS
WHY IT EXISTS
Barangay User Management
Authorized DRRM Administrators create Barangay accounts and issue system-generated credentials.
Keeps account creation under the level responsible for citywide user administration.
Barangay Profile Creation
Consolidate verified Purok profile records into the Barangay profile.
Builds the higher-level baseline from its constituent Purok data instead of re-entering it.
Barangay Profile Verification
An authorized DRRM reviewer checks the consolidated profile against references and records the decision.
Adds a higher-level review before the Barangay profile is treated as verified.
Source: revised paper, Specific Objectives 309–311 and Table 3

SLIDE 23
========================================================================
PROPOSED SYSTEM MODULES: DETAILED
Barangay incident reporting modules
MODULE
HOW IT WORKS
WHY IT EXISTS
Barangay Incident Reporting
Consolidate eligible verified Purok reports, add Barangay-level assessment, and return unverified source reports for correction.
Produces a multi-Purok incident account with more aggregated effects.
Barangay Incident Report Verification
DRRM reviewers check new or modified submissions, compare sources, flag conflicts, and verify, partially verify, return or reject with a reason.
Only reviewed Barangay reports are eligible for SitRep consolidation.
Barangay Incident Report History
Retrieve current and earlier versions of consolidated Barangay reports.
Supports review, correction and tracing of SitRep source data over time.
Source: revised paper, Specific Objectives 312–314 and Table 3

PROPOSED SYSTEM MODULES: DETAILED
DRRM administration and hazard modules
MODULE
HOW IT WORKS
WHY IT EXISTS
System User Management
DRRM Administrators view, edit, activate or suspend accounts across all three levels.
Controls access while preserving authorship and approval history when an account changes.
Hazard Creation
Authorized users record hazard details and classify entries against the participating organization's CDRA types, adding a type when needed.
Keeps hazard terminology consistent with local assessment practice. The system records the chosen type and does not independently classify events.

SLIDE 25

PROPOSED SYSTEM MODULES: DETAILED
DRRM reporting and analytics modules
MODULE
HOW IT WORKS
WHY IT EXISTS
Situation Report Generation
Select an incident and cutoff, retrieve verified Barangay reports, build an editable template-based draft, then route it for authorized review, approval and export.
Reduces repeated summarization while retaining source attribution and human authority.
Situation Report Historical Catalog
Search, filter, compare, export, correct or supersede reports while retaining prior approved versions and record controls.
Preserves continuity and supports audit, retention and later retrieval.
System Dashboard
Show role-specific incident, population, report-status, verification, SitRep-coverage and processing summaries, with filters and last-update time.
Gives each level relevant operational visibility and distinguishes provisional from verified data.
