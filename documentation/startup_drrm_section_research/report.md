## Institutional authority and electronic disaster records

### Primary Sources

The principal authorities are [Republic Act No. 10121](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2010/ra_10121_2010.html), the [2024 NDRRM Operations Center SOPG](https://ndrrmc.gov.ph/attachments/article/4125/NDRRMOC_SOPG_2024_EDITION.pdf), [Republic Act No. 8792](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2000/ra_8792_2000.html), [Republic Act No. 9470](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9470_2007.html), and [Republic Act No. 12254](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2025/ra_12254_2025.html). Operation L!STO materials supplement these authorities as operational preparedness guidance for LGUs.

### Evidence Quality and Caveats

The statutes and official SOPG are primary legal and operational sources. They establish public responsibilities, report stages, records duties, and electronic-governance directions, but they do not mandate this particular platform or confer government authority on its startup operator. Operation L!STO supports preparedness workflows but should not be cited as prescribing every proposed electronic field or route.

### Scope Inclusions

The study should include organization and jurisdiction identifiers, human-controlled report states, progressive Initial/Progress/Terminal/Final records where applicable, source attribution, versions, approval evidence, export, retention information, and local SitRep preparation. It should preserve the public office's authority over official submission and records custody.

### Scope Exclusions and Dependencies

The system should exclude autonomous declarations, resource allocation, official public warnings, and unsupported claims of national-system integration. Legal acceptance of electronic outputs depends on the responsible agency's procedure, while retention and disposal depend on authorized records schedules and National Archives requirements.

### Objective Requirements and Metrics

Module objectives should test correct role and jurisdiction boundaries, eligible-record consolidation, mapping to applicable SitRep categories, exclusion of rejected or superseded inputs, version preservation, and export completeness. A generated file must remain distinguishable from an approved or officially transmitted report.

### Significance Mechanisms and Beneficiaries

Local reporters, reviewers, DRRM organizations, records personnel, and affected communities may benefit from more structured, attributable, and retrievable information. The causal mechanism is improved organization and traceability, not automatic accuracy or improved disaster outcomes.

### Authority and Responsibility Matrix

Participating organizations control lawful purpose, users, jurisdictions, verification, approval, disclosure, official submission, custody, retention, and disposition. The startup controls only contracted technical functions such as hosting, maintenance, security, backup, recovery, support, export, and transition assistance.

### Data Lifecycle and Interoperability

Records should preserve stable identifiers, source, geography, event and submission times, status, decision history, versions, and applicable retention controls. Documented schemas and exports can prepare for later exchange but do not establish technical, semantic, organizational, or legal interoperability.

### Risk, Continuity, and Inclusion

The design must retain human approval, limit disclosure, support attributable corrections, preserve public records, and maintain contingency channels during outages. Provider dependence requires backup, restoration, export, and exit arrangements.

## Startup service governance, privacy, security, and continuity

### Primary Sources

The principal sources are [Republic Act No. 10173](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), its [Implementing Rules and Regulations](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/), relevant National Privacy Commission issuances, [Republic Act No. 12009](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_12009_2024.html), its procurement framework, and Republic Act No. 12254.

### Evidence Quality and Caveats

These authorities support privacy accountability, outsourced-processing controls, public procurement, continuity, and electronic-government collaboration. Their applicability depends on the final service, contract, data flows, customer classification, and actual operation. A prototype cannot claim compliance merely because corresponding features are planned.

### Scope Inclusions

Include customer onboarding, organization isolation, role administration, controlled support access, processing documentation, incident contacts, backup and restoration, customer export, and offboarding. The scope should identify platform administration as separate from DRRM operational authority.

### Scope Exclusions and Dependencies

Exclude claims of procurement eligibility, production-grade security, guaranteed availability, commercial viability, or automatic legal compliance. These depend on contracts, funding, privacy and security assessments, subprocessors, service levels, customer governance, and production evidence.

### Objective Requirements and Metrics

Test at least two independently populated organizations across list, direct-record, create, update, approval, dashboard, file, support, and export paths. Test restoration against record counts, relationships, attachments, values, roles, and organization boundaries. Verify that offboarding exports are complete and independently understandable.

### Significance Mechanisms and Beneficiaries

Customers may avoid duplicated custom systems and receive common maintenance and support. The startup may gain a reusable service model. Benefits are conditional on affordability, adoption, security, support quality, contractual accountability, portability, and service continuity.

### Authority and Responsibility Matrix

Customers determine processing purposes and operational decisions. The startup processes and protects information under documented authority, discloses incidents, controls staff access, manages approved subprocessors, and returns or lawfully retains data at exit. Shared responsibilities must be stated in contracts and operating procedures.

### Data Lifecycle and Interoperability

Collect only necessary data; record source and purpose; restrict access; log material actions; apply retention instructions; preserve export metadata and relationships; revoke access during offboarding; and dispose of residual processor copies only when legally authorized.

### Risk, Continuity, and Inclusion

Key risks are cross-organization leakage, excessive support access, data loss, outages, vendor failure, inaccessible interfaces, and exclusion of users with weak connectivity. Controls include least privilege, organization-scoped access, logs, tested backups, incident response, export, transition assistance, accessible responsive design, and retained non-web contingencies.

## Information quality, inclusion, and measurable evaluation

### Primary Sources

The evidence base includes [ISO/IEC 25010:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html), [ISO/IEC 25012:2008](https://www.iso.org/standard/35736.html), [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), Brooke's original [System Usability Scale](https://hci-studies.org/methods-and-measures/downloads/SUS_Brooke1996.pdf), and primary academic studies on disaster-information quality, verification, volunteered geographic information, and scenario evaluation.

### Evidence Quality and Caveats

Standards define quality models and testable criteria but do not prove that this product improves disaster outcomes. Academic findings come from different hazards, countries, platforms, and populations and therefore justify design controls and hypotheses rather than project-specific effect sizes. Full ISO provisions should be claimed only when actually consulted.

### Scope Inclusions

Include provenance, verification status, timestamps, currentness, location, duplicates, versions, completeness rules, correction history, accessible task flows, low-bandwidth testing, and representative scenarios involving incomplete, conflicting, stale, corrected, and inaccessible information.

### Scope Exclusions and Dependencies

Exclude claims that form completeness equals accuracy, online reports represent the entire population, automated scanning proves accessibility, or a usability score proves operational effectiveness. Results depend on representative users, reference data, reviewers, devices, connectivity, assistive-technology testing, and declared scenarios.

### Objective Requirements and Metrics

Measure only what is defined and tested: task completion, critical errors, time on task, completeness, valid-value rate, duplicate performance where implemented, provenance coverage, currentness, verification decisions against reference cases, SitRep aggregation accuracy, WCAG criteria, restoration, and organization isolation. SUS is a 0–100 perception score, not a percentage or universal pass mark.

### Significance Mechanisms and Beneficiaries

Personnel may benefit when provenance and status reduce ambiguity and search effort; communities may benefit when authorized information is more current and accessible. These mechanisms require accurate inputs, timely reviewers, training, inclusion, and connection to official procedures. Possible harms include false confidence, automation bias, exclusion, and sensitive-data disclosure.

### Authority and Responsibility Matrix

Customers define authoritative sources, meanings, verification rules, risk tolerance, accessibility needs, and publication authority. The startup implements controls, preserves provenance, operates support, and reports defects and incidents. Independent evaluators should adjudicate reference cases where feasible.

### Data Lifecycle and Interoperability

Assign stable identifiers; preserve source, timestamps, location precision, status, reviewer, reasons, and change history; supersede rather than silently overwrite corrected assertions; version schemas and rules; expose missingness and denominators; and export records with definitions and audit metadata.

### Risk, Continuity, and Inclusion

Apply least privilege, organization isolation, privacy minimization, protected logs, backup and restore testing, accessibility evaluation, low-bandwidth scenarios, and contingency intake. Report results by role, device, connection, language, or access group when the sample permits, since combined averages can conceal exclusion.
