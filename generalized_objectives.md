# 1.3 Objectives

## General Objective

To design, develop, and evaluate a centralized, role-based, responsive web-based Disaster Situation Record Management and Situation Report Generation System that can be operated by the startup for multiple participating DRRM organizations, while allowing each organization to manage its own users, jurisdictions, reporting workflow, records, and local Situation Reports for floods, typhoons, and fires within enforced organizational boundaries.

## Specific Objectives

1. **Develop responsive, role-specific web access.**
   Develop one browser-based application whose navigation, forms, tables, dashboards, and report views adapt to supported desktop, tablet, and smartphone screens without requiring a separately installed mobile application. Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA for the complete role-based processes in scope, including semantic relationships, contrast, reflow at 320 CSS pixels except for genuinely two-dimensional content, keyboard access, visible and unobscured focus, minimum target size, labels, error identification, redundant-entry reduction, accessible authentication, programmatic names and roles, and status messages. Evaluate with automated checks and manual keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, portrait/landscape, touch, software-keyboard, error-recovery, and task-based tests on supported devices and browsers. WCAG conformance and viewport checks measure accessibility and adaptation; they do not by themselves prove operational usability ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [ICTO-NCDA Accessible Website Design Guidelines](https://ncda.gov.ph/disability-laws/joint-circulars/accessible-website-design-guidelines/)).

   For every applicable Level A and Level AA success criterion, record the tested page or workflow, evaluation method, result as pass, fail, or not applicable, and the rationale for any exclusion. Accessibility conformance should be determined across the complete in-scope process rather than calculated as an average percentage of passed checks. Mobile evaluation should also include orientation changes, software-keyboard obstruction, zoom and reflow, interrupted requests, and completion of reporting tasks on the actual supported browser and device combinations.

2. **Support configurable multi-organization operation and data isolation.**
   Develop organization onboarding, activation, suspension, reactivation, configuration, export, and offboarding functions for participating DRRM organizations. Allow each organization to maintain its own jurisdiction hierarchy, local role assignments, approval route, terminology, and SitRep template while retaining a common application codebase and core data model. Preserve the organization configuration, workflow version, and template version that applied to every submitted or approved report so that historical records remain reproducible after settings change.

   Derive organization context from the authenticated account and enforce it in object authorization, database queries, files, caches, searches, dashboards, exports, and audit records. Verify isolation by creating at least two independently populated organizations and testing list, direct-object, creation, update, approval, file, dashboard, export, and platform-support paths. The acceptance criterion for the executed isolation cases is zero unauthorized cross-organization disclosures or modifications, with the number of test cases and any failures reported. This criterion applies only to the tested paths and should not be stated as proof of absolute isolation ([OWASP Multi-Tenant Security Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multi_Tenant_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html)).

3. **Implement secure user, privacy, and access management.**
   Develop registration, account verification, authentication, session management, one-way password hashing, account status controls, and deny-by-default role and object authorization for platform administrators, organization administrators, reporters, reviewers, verifiers, approvers, and authorized viewers. Platform administration must remain separate from operational DRRM authority. Support personnel should have no standing access to customer records; any required access must be approved by the organization, limited by role, purpose, record scope, and duration, logged, reviewed, and revoked when the support task ends.

   Apply TLS in transit, risk-appropriate protection at rest, CSRF and injection defenses, rate-limited authentication, session-token renewal after authentication, logout invalidation, access removal for disabled accounts, and logs that record who performed what action, when, and where without copying credentials, tokens, or unnecessary health details. Verify permitted actions and negative cases such as cross-role access, direct-record access, disabled accounts, failed authorization, support-access expiry, and session reuse. Before evaluation, document the Privacy Impact Assessment, lawful purposes and data flows, privacy notice, retention classes, breach response, backup and restoration procedure, and recovery result. Under an outsourcing arrangement, also document the organization and startup responsibilities for processing instructions, confidentiality, subprocessors, audits, data-subject assistance, return, retention, and lawful disposal. Use NPC Circular No. 2023-06 and OWASP ASVS 5.0 as governance and verification references, not as unsupported blanket compliance claims ([Data Privacy Act IRR](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/); [NPC Circular No. 2023-06](https://privacy.gov.ph/npc-issues-circulars-to-strengthen-personal-data-protection-in-ph/); [OWASP ASVS](https://owasp.org/www-project-application-security-verification-standard/)).

   Define recovery-time and recovery-point objectives before testing based on risk and service requirements. Measure achieved recovery time from the declared disruption until service and restored data are validated. Determine the achieved recovery point from the latest committed record successfully restored and report the resulting data-loss interval. Restoration testing should verify record counts, relationships, attachments, selected values, role restrictions, and organization isolation instead of treating successful backup creation as proof of recoverability ([NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29)).

4. **Standardize initial and barangay disaster reporting.**
   Develop structured forms for initial and detailed reports that capture incident, organization, jurisdiction, location, time, affected-population, casualty, infrastructure-damage, immediate-need, action-taken, source, and uncertainty data appropriate to the report stage. Include required-field, format, range, controlled-value, logical-consistency, and duplicate checks while allowing explicit unknown or not-yet-verified values and narrative remarks where an urgent initial report is legitimately incomplete.

   Measure data quality separately by dimension. Field completeness is the number of applicable required elements populated divided by all applicable required elements. Valid-value rate is the number of values satisfying declared format, domain, and range rules divided by all values tested. Consistency rate is the number of declared cross-field and cross-record rules satisfied divided by all rules evaluated. Duplicate-free rate is the number of unique incident records divided by all records tested after defining the incident-matching rule. Timeliness is the difference between the event or observation time and system receipt time and should be summarized by reporting stage. Accuracy is the number of values agreeing with an independent reference divided by all values compared; it cannot be inferred from form validation alone. First-pass acceptance, defined as submissions reaching verification without return for required-field or logic correction divided by all submissions reviewed, should be reported as a workflow measure rather than as a substitute for completeness ([ISO/IEC 25012:2008](https://www.iso.org/standard/35736.html)).

5. **Implement a traceable verification and correction workflow.**
   Develop report states and permitted transitions for preliminary submission, review, return for correction, validation, rejection, consolidation, amendment, and archival. Preserve the source organization and jurisdiction, original submission time, incident time, reviewer or verifier, decision and reason, prior authorized version, and an append-only history of material events; do not silently overwrite decision records. Store timestamps with an explicit timezone or in UTC. Measure transition correctness as permitted state transitions completed correctly divided by all attempted tested transitions. Measure decision-record completeness as sampled decisions containing the required actor, organization, timestamp, outcome, reason, and source-version reference divided by all decisions sampled. Define verification turnaround as the decision timestamp minus the submission timestamp and report verified, returned, and rejected outcomes separately. Compare equivalent existing- and proposed-process scenarios using median turnaround, first-pass acceptance, correction effort, handoffs, queue delay, and unresolved backlog. These are evaluation measures, not guaranteed improvements.

6. **Develop local SitRep consolidation and generation.**
   Develop a module that retrieves eligible validated records within the authorized organization and jurisdiction, aggregates applicable barangay data, and produces a reviewable local SitRep mapped to the NDRRM Operations Center categories: prevailing situation, preparedness measures, response actions, consolidated effects, issues and concerns, and recommendations where applicable. The prototype assists local preparation and does not itself issue an official national NDRRMC SitRep. Measure mapping completeness as correctly populated applicable required SitRep elements divided by all applicable required elements. Measure aggregation accuracy as aggregate values equal to independently calculated expected values divided by all aggregate values tested. Measure provenance traceability as sampled output values whose source organization, jurisdiction, eligible source record, source version, and verification decision can be followed without a broken link divided by all output values sampled. Include exclusion tests confirming that preliminary, rejected, superseded, wrong-organization, and otherwise ineligible records are not consolidated ([NDRRM Operations Center SOPG, 2024 Edition](https://ndrrmc.gov.ph/attachments/article/4125/NDRRMOC_SOPG_2024_EDITION.pdf)).

7. **Develop searchable records, dashboards, and audit support.**
   Develop a SitRep catalog with authorized viewing, searching, filtering, version identification, amendment while preserving prior authorized versions, export, and chronological retrieval. Associate records with their organization, jurisdiction, configuration version, retention class, disposition status, and legal or archival hold where applicable; permit disposal only through authorized records-management procedures. Develop dashboard summaries for report volume, affected population, validation status, and processing activity using organization-scoped database values and minimum necessary detail. Maintain event logs for security-relevant and record-changing actions.

   Measure catalog retrieval success as queries returning the predefined expected record set and ordering divided by all queries tested. Measure dashboard accuracy as displayed aggregate values matching independent expected results divided by all values tested. Measure audit completeness as required events containing actor or service, organization, action, target, outcome, and timestamp divided by all required events sampled. For organization offboarding, measure export completeness as required organization-owned records, metadata, versions, and attachments included divided by all expected items, and measure relationship integrity as resolving exported references divided by all references tested. Export should not be described as successful migration unless a separate import or independent-use test is performed ([RA 9470 IRR](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/IRR-of-R.A.-9470.pdf)).

8. **Evaluate functional suitability, interaction capability, reliability, performance efficiency, security, compatibility, flexibility, and accessibility.**
   Conduct unit, integration, system, security, and user acceptance testing with realistic role-based tasks and representative data. Before testing, specify participating organizations, roles, jurisdictions, hazards, supported browsers and versions, devices and viewports, network conditions, role-action matrix, workflow and template versions, expected aggregation outputs, dataset size, response-time conditions, backup and recovery targets, applicable WCAG 2.2 AA criteria, and a pass rule for each measure. Use selected current characteristics from ISO/IEC 25010:2023 as a reference model, not as a certification checklist or source of universal thresholds. The 2023 model uses interaction capability rather than the older usability characteristic and treats adaptability within flexibility; only characteristics actually evaluated should be claimed ([ISO/IEC 25010:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html)).

   Define task success as completion to the correct end state without a critical error and report unaided and assisted success separately. Report task time for successful tasks using the median and interquartile range when appropriate, with the treatment of failed tasks stated. Record errors by consequence, including cosmetic defects, recoverable workflow errors, incorrect aggregation, unauthorized disclosure, and loss of record integrity. Define availability for the stated observation window as scheduled service time minus unplanned unavailable time, divided by scheduled service time, and disclose how maintenance and partial failures are treated. Measure client-observed response time at declared percentiles under stated data, concurrency, device, and network conditions. A short prototype test must not be presented as proof of production service-level availability.

   Score SUS after realistic tasks using the original calculation: subtract one from responses to odd-numbered items, subtract even-numbered responses from five, sum the adjusted values, and multiply by 2.5. Report sample size, respondent roles, central tendency, and dispersion. SUS is a 0-100 composite perception score, not a percentage of usability, a diagnostic subscale, or a universal pass criterion. Interpret it together with task success, errors, accessibility results, data quality, and qualitative feedback ([Brooke, 1996](https://hci-studies.org/methods-and-measures/downloads/SUS_Brooke1996.pdf); [Bangor et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447310802205776)).

## Objective Boundaries

The objectives cover the responsive web application, centralized database, multi-organization administration and isolation, controlled configuration, organization lifecycle, role controls, structured reporting, verification, SitRep generation, catalog, dashboard, audit trail, export, recovery, and evaluation. They do not include a native mobile application, offline synchronization, SMS fallback, public alerting, emergency dispatch, predictive analytics, or implemented integration with external government systems. Structured exports, stable identifiers, and documented field definitions may support future interoperability, but they should not be described as an existing national-system integration. ZCDRRMO may remain an original study setting or potential pilot organization; results from one organization should not be generalized to all DRRM offices without additional evidence.
