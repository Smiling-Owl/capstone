# 1.3 Objectives

## General Objective

The general objective of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate a centralized, role-based, responsive Online Disaster Situation Report Generation and Record Management System as a startup-operated service for participating disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) organizations. The system will support the traceable progression of local disaster information from initial reporting and human verification to local Situation Report (SitRep) preparation, approval, retrieval, and authorized export for floods, typhoons, and fires. It will use one maintainable application while separating each organization's users, jurisdictions, workflows, configurations, and records and preserving the participating organization's authority over operational decisions and official submissions.

## Specific Objectives

Specifically, the study aims to:

1. Develop a **User and Organization Management Module** that allows the startup to onboard, configure, activate, suspend, and offboard participating DRRM organizations and enables each organization to manage its jurisdiction hierarchy, authorized users, and role assignments. The module will support distinct organization administrators, reporters, reviewers or verifiers, approvers, and viewers; apply least-privilege and organization-scoped access; preserve the authorship of records when an account is disabled; and record security-relevant changes. Platform administration will remain separate from public authority: startup personnel may maintain the service and provide specifically authorized, purpose-limited, time-limited, and logged support, but may not independently verify incidents, approve SitReps, or disclose customer records.

2. Develop a **Disaster Reporting Module** that enables authorized reporters to create, save, submit, and update structured initial and detailed disaster reports. The module will capture a stable incident and submission identifier; organization and jurisdiction; hazard type; occurrence, observation, and submission times; location; source; narrative; affected population; casualties; damage; immediate needs; actions taken; attachments where authorized; and the verification or uncertainty state of the information. It will distinguish zero, unknown, and not applicable values, allow incomplete but clearly marked initial observations when operationally necessary, and apply required-field, format, range, consistency, and possible-duplicate checks. It will display whether a report is in draft, being submitted, successfully acknowledged, failed, or requires reconciliation after interruption so that retry does not silently create duplicate records.

3. Develop a **Disaster Report Verification Module** that enables authorized personnel to triage and examine submitted reports; compare sources and versions; request clarification or correction; flag duplicates and conflicting values; and validate, partially validate, return, or reject a report with a recorded reason. The module will preserve the original submitted version, source organization, jurisdiction, responsible actor, timestamps, evidence references, decision, reason, and subsequent corrections in an append-only material history. Automated rules may identify inconsistencies or suggest possible duplicates, but the system will not determine which field account is true or confer verification authority on the startup. Concurrent or late corrections will require an explicit reconciliation decision instead of silently overwriting a reviewed record.

4. Develop a **SitRep Generation Module** that retrieves only eligible report versions within the authenticated organization's authorized jurisdiction and assembles a reviewable local SitRep using the applicable categories of the 2024 NDRRM Operations Center Standard Operating Procedures and Guidelines. Depending on the participating organization's configured workflow, the output will identify its report type or stage, report number, operational or reporting period, cut-off or “as of” time, prevailing situation, preparedness measures, response actions, consolidated effects, issues and concerns, recommendations, included sources, unresolved discrepancies, template version, generated version, and approval status. Every sampled consolidated value should remain traceable to an eligible source record and verification decision. Generation will produce a draft for authorized human review and approval and will not by itself create an official national NDRRMC issuance or transmit a report to an external agency.

5. Develop a **SitRep Catalog Module** that maintains the controlled reference data and historical records needed to interpret reports consistently. It will support authorized management of hazards, geographic units, organizations, report types, statuses, impact categories, units of measure, and other implemented terms with effective dates or versions where changes affect historical interpretation. It will also allow authorized users to view, search, filter, compare, export, correct, and supersede SitReps while preserving prior approved versions, source relationships, classification, retention information, legal or archival holds, and audit history. Offboarding will provide the participating organization with a documented and usable export of its records, metadata, versions, relationships, and authorized attachments, subject to applicable public-record retention and lawful-disposition requirements.

6. Develop a **Dashboard Module** that presents organization- and role-scoped summaries of disaster incidents, affected populations, report status, verification activity, SitRep coverage, and processing time using records available to the authenticated user. The dashboard will distinguish provisional from verified values, disclose the applicable filters, reporting period, last update, missing data, and denominator where relevant, and provide drill-down to permitted source records. Charts will include textual or tabular equivalents and will not rely on color alone. The dashboard will support monitoring of recorded information; it will not be represented as a forecasting, dispatch, resource-allocation, or automatic decision-making system, and absence of a digital report will not be interpreted as absence of disaster impact.

7. Evaluate the six modules through functional, integration, security, accessibility, recovery, and role-based user testing using declared organizations, roles, datasets, devices, browsers, network conditions, and disaster scenarios. Evaluation will include at least two independently populated organizations for cross-organization access tests; seeded valid, incomplete, conflicting, duplicate, late, corrected, and ineligible reports; normal and interrupted submission scenarios; backup restoration; customer export; and complete reporting, verification, generation, retrieval, and dashboard tasks. Measures will include task completion and critical errors, time on task, applicable-field completeness, valid-value rate, correction frequency, verification turnaround, permitted state-transition success, SitRep aggregation accuracy, source-traceability coverage, retrieval and dashboard accuracy, prohibited access attempts blocked, restoration result, export completeness, applicable WCAG 2.2 results, and user feedback. Where feasible, the same scripted records and tasks will be performed using the current paper, spreadsheet, messaging, or email process to provide a baseline. Results will be reported with their numerator, denominator, task conditions, failures, and sample characteristics rather than combined into an unsupported claim of overall effectiveness.

The objectives exclude a native mobile application, full offline synchronization, SMS or radio integration, predictive analytics, automated public warnings, emergency dispatch, independent truth determination, and live integration with national government systems. The responsive web application depends on compatible devices, electricity, internet connectivity, hosting availability, trained personnel, and authorized human review. Prototype usability, intention to use, or technical test results will not be treated as proof of procurement eligibility, willingness to pay, commercial viability, nationwide suitability, production availability, or improved public-safety outcomes.
